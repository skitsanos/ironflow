# IF-266 — Flow-load admission test gives the detached parse a fixed 2 s restore window

## Evidence and boundary

CI run 44752334482 on `develop` commit `0075fcd8` (2026-11-20), job
`Test (macos-latest)`, failed in
`tests/test_flow_load_admission.rs::a_blocked_parse_makes_a_concurrent_validation_fail_fast`
with `flow-load permit was not restored after the detached parse settled:
Elapsed(())`. A re-run of the same job passed, and the test had passed in the
previous 14 `develop` runs. Earlier stabilizations, including IF-097, widened
other waits in this test but not this one.

Read: `validate_flow` takes a flow-load permit and hands it to
`supervise_flow_load`, a detached task that owns the permit until the parse
future returns. Aborting the request drops only the waiter, so the blocking
`while true do end` parse keeps running until the Lua hook reports
`IRONFLOW_LUA_MAX_SECONDS` (1 s here), measured from when the limits are
applied inside the blocking worker. The supervisor then drops the permit. The
release is bounded by the budget plus worker scheduling; the product does not
delay it.

The test polled for the restored permit under a fixed 2 s timeout starting
shortly after the parse is spawned, leaving about 1 s of slack that a stalled
hosted runner can use up.

Reproduced locally:

- Unloaded, the permit is observed back about 1.01 s after the parse was
  spawned. With the CPU oversubscribed threefold and sixfold, 30 runs restored
  it within 1.12 s and none failed, so plain CPU contention on this machine did
  not reproduce the failure.
- Pausing the test process with `SIGSTOP` for 2.5 s, starting 0.4 s into the
  test, reproduced the exact CI panic in 5 of 5 runs of the original test. That
  is a simulated runner stall, not the CI runner's own condition, which was not
  observed.

## Outcome and validation

Only the test changed:

- The restore deadline is the configured Lua budget plus a 10 s
  loaded-runner allowance, the same allowance IF-097 gave the slow run in this
  test. It still fails if the permit is never restored.
- New lower bound: the permit must not be observed back before the Lua budget
  has elapsed since the parse was spawned. The budget starts only after spawn,
  so this cannot flake, and together with the existing refusal right after the
  abort it proves that admission is held for the whole detached parse and not
  released when the client disconnects.

Validation on 2026-11-20 with the fixed test:

- `cargo test --test flow_load_admission`: 20 of 20 unloaded; 20 of 20 with
  the CPU oversubscribed threefold and 10 of 10 sixfold.
- The same `SIGSTOP` stall that failed the original test passed 5 of 5 runs
  at 2.5 s and 3 of 3 at 6 s.
- `cargo fmt --all -- --check`, `python3 -B scripts/check_module_size.py` and
  `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings` pass.

Unchanged: the two 1 s refusal-observation waits must see a permit while a
parse holds it, so widening them would not help a stall that outlasts the
parse. Neither has failed in CI.
